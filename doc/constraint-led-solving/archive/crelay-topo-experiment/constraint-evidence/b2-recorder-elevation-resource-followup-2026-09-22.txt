QUESTION
Could a second recorder cycle retain a live actor at location20 while its
physical gate2 view is open, by using a ghost-held tray as the support?

CANDIDATE MECHANISM
The recorder semantics admit the relevant split only conditionally:

1. A ghost may toggle switch1 on. That changes both its recording switch
   memory and the ordinary physical switch, so both the recording and physical
   blower views become active.
2. If both agents are already on blower1, propagation raises them to
   location20. A ghost holding tray1* there would make that tray a support at
   the ghost holder's level.
3. The live agent may mount a ghost-held tray during recording. This is the
   one directional cross-layer support exception.
4. A live toggle of switch1 off changes only the ordinary physical switch.
   Gate2 would then be open in the live view while the ghost's recording
   blower remained active. The ghost and the live rider would not satisfy
   DROP-OCCUPANTS!'s unsupported-occupant condition.

Thus the controller split itself is not the missing resource. A ghost-held
tray already at location20 would retain a live actor there through the
physical switch-off boundary.

WHY IT DOES NOT REALIZE A B2 RESOURCE
At the B1 endpoint, starting the second cycle copies tray1 onto plate1 as
tray1*, at location2. The ghost agent begins at location1. To carry tray1*,
the ghost must first pick it up. That removes its recording-side pressure
from plate1, immediately closing recording gate1.

The archived B1 witness establishes the relevant recorded crossing:
location1 reaches location7 through gate1, and location7 returns to
location2 through gate1. The current B2 baseline analysis likewise depends
on tray1* keeping recording gate1 open for the ghost to leave this region.
Once the ghost holds tray1*, its required recorded passage toward blower1 is
not supplied. A live tray cannot repair the recording view, and the ghost
cannot manipulate the live tray; recorder manipulation is same-side only.

No other current support can replace tray1*:

* box1 is the still-unreached cargo in location6;
* a grounded tray is inert;
* a tray held by the live actor cannot support its own holder and cannot be
  carried by the ghost;
* the authored ladders end at ground configurations and do not reach
  location20 or the gate2 alcove; and
* the blower remains the only authored elevation transition to location20.

The split-lift sequence therefore identifies a conditional mechanism but not
an available elevation resource from B1. It does not reopen B2, replace the
existing cutoff-12 bound, establish a B2 endpoint, or authorize runtime
work. A future reopening still requires a separately justified way to bring
an independent, propagation-safe support to the elevated approach without
relying on the tray1*/gate1 dependency.
