2. AGREED PROBLEM AND ANSWER

Initial arrangement:
- Agent and box1 on the ground in area1; box2 on the ground in area2.
- Plates1 and 2 in area1; plate3 in area3; all initially empty.
- Gate1 joins areas1/2, gate2 joins areas1/3, gate3 joins areas3/4.
- PlateN controls gateN; all gates initially closed. Goal: agent in area4.

Rules confirmed by the user:
- One agent can carry at most one box; pickup requires empty hands.
- All boxes are interchangeable in relevant abilities and action costs.
- Pick up a local box; place a held box on local ground or an empty local plate.
- Carrying a box through an open gate is allowed.
- Ground storage in each area is unlimited and never blocks movement.
- Walking within an area is unrestricted and uncounted.
- A plate holds at most one occupant: the agent or a placed box.
- Occupying a plate opens its gate; vacating it closes the gate immediately.
- Each controlling plate is in one of the two areas joined by its gate.
- An action is a pickup, placement, gate crossing, or step onto/off a plate.
  Each counts as one. Ordinary internal walking counts as none.
- Return one globally shortest path; tied paths need not all be returned.

Interpretation used throughout: actions are sequential and local; crossing
requires the gate to remain open for passage. Plate and gate are distinct
locations in the diagram. No remote manipulation, delayed closure, other agents,
or additional mechanisms are introduced. Initial locations above come from the
diagram and the user's agreed paraphrase.

Preserving requirements is preferred. Downstream advisors may propose concrete
tradeoffs, identifying benefit, lost guarantee, evidence, and scope. No relaxation
of the shortest-path objective has been accepted.

3. SEARCH REPRESENTATION AND CHOICES

Static information: areas, gate endpoints, each gate's controlling plate and its
endpoint area, goal area, and the supplied objects. Preserve the given layout.

Sufficient conceptual state for the reduced search:
- Agent's area.
- Number of loose boxes on the ground in each area.
- Which plates hold boxes.
- Whether the agent carries a box.

Conserve the total number of boxes. Derive gate openness from occupied plates;
there is no persistent gate memory. Box names may be retained in the executable
representation and representative path, but must not create semantically distinct
search arrangements. No elapsed time, route history, or exact walking coordinates
are needed under these rules. Accumulated action count is a path cost, not a new
physical arrangement; duplicate handling must preserve a cheaper arrival.

Generate local pickups with empty hands, placements of the carried box onto local
ground or an empty local plate, and crossings along actual open incident gates.
Each generated transition represents exactly one counted action. One pickup
choice suffices for identical loose boxes in the same area. Do not impose a fixed
box order or prohibit box recovery.

Alternative: retain named box locations throughout and use canonical state keys
for equivalence. The spec advisor selects the concrete representation; either

4. JUSTIFIED REDUCTIONS AND TERMINATION

R1 — Omit agent stepping onto/off plates for this problem type.
The user's latest instruction identifies this reduction; the reasoning supports
it for scaled instances under the same rules, not merely for the ten-action case.
The lone agent cannot cross the controlled gate while standing on its separate
plate. Stepping off closes it immediately. While standing there, any useful local
pickup or placement can instead be performed from the area's ground: within-area
movement is free, and local handling does not require the gate to be open.
Occupying the plate cannot enable placing a box on it because capacity is one.
There is no other agent or process to exploit the temporary opening. Goal success
requires reaching an area, not standing on a plate. Removing plate visits therefore
preserves useful transitions and strictly reduces cost when such visits occur.

Keep agent activation as an intended physical ability, but omit its actions and
standing-on-plate state from this shortest-path search. Whether a carrying agent
could stand on a plate is irrelevant to the reduced model and is not an intake
blocker. This is an optimization model, not a complete simulator of every ability.
Reopen R1 if goal, timing, locality, action costs, or mechanisms change; merely
adding more objects and connections under the same rules does not invalidate it.

R2 — Ignore permutations of interchangeable box identities.
Exchanging labels preserves legality, goals, and costs. Equivalent arrangements
may share search work. Retain a representative path that names real boxes
consistently. This removes label permutations, not different spatial allocations.
Do not assume distinct areas or gates are interchangeable without a separate proof.

R3 — Remove positive-cost cycles in complete arrangements.
An identical arrangement has identical legal futures. Deleting a cycle shortens
a path, so ancestor cycle exclusion preserves an optimum. For graph sharing,
retain the least-cost arrival and correctly handle cheaper later arrivals.

Finite areas, plates, and boxes yield finitely many reduced arrangements; all
retained actions have positive unit cost. Ancestor exclusion bounds a path by the
finite arrangements, or suitable graph handling prevents indefinite re-exploration.
The count is not a guarantee of practical runtime.

5. SUPPLIED INSTANCE: TEN-ACTION OPTIMUM

1. Pick up box1.
2. Place box1 on plate1.
3. Cross gate1 into area2.
4. Pick up box2.
5. Cross gate1 back into area1 carrying box2.
6. Place box2 on plate2.
7. Pick up box1 from plate1.
8. Cross gate2 into area3 carrying box1.
9. Place box1 on plate3.
10. Cross gate3 into area4.

Matching lower bound: gate2 needs a box left on plate2 while another reaches
plate3, so box2 must be retrieved. Retrieval requires two gate1 crossings, followed
by gate2 and gate3: at least four crossings. Each of the three plates must receive
a box: at least three placements. Initially no box is carried, and each placement
uses a held box: at least three pickups. These are disjoint action counts, totaling
ten. R1 rules out obtaining a useful crossing from the agent's own plate occupancy.
The witness attains the bound. This is a deduction, not an executed search result.

A cutoff of 10 is justified only for this supplied instance by its witness. It is
not a general family limit. A larger instance needs its own justified coverage.

6. SCALING: NAVIGATION COUPLED TO REUSABLE RESOURCES

The user's decomposition is useful: a fixed maze of possible connections, current
gate access, and spatially distributed reusable boxes. Placing a box commits it;
recovery frees it but closes an edge. Total box count alone is insufficient.
Solving a geometric route first and allocating boxes afterward can lose feasibility
or optimality. A detour may retrieve boxes or avoid expensive gate commitments.

Growth dimensions and required responses:
- Boxes: use R2 and counts for loose boxes; preserve carrying/plate capacities.
- Areas: preserve the compact area abstraction; more areas create more possible
  box distributions and may increase transport distance.
- Plate/gate pairs: derive gate status; track commitments, recovery access, and
  which endpoint holds each plate. Do not reserve one box permanently per gate.
- Connections and loops: generate incident moves; equivalent arrangements may
  recur through different routes. Evaluate cost-aware graph sharing against its
  memory cost. More connections can also shorten routes; density alone is not
  a difficulty measure.
- Interactions: topology, resource distribution, and gate commitments jointly
  determine useful choices. Locally cheapest segments need not compose optimally.

For A areas, P plates, B boxes, k occupied plates and c carried boxes (0 or 1),
there are binomial(B-k-c+A-1, A-1) loose-box distributions for each legal occupied
plate subset. This is a combinatorial count, not a reachable-state measurement.
The previous labelled full-model bound (A+P)*(A+P+1)^B is retained in history;
the reduced model removes label permutations and agent plate occupancy.

Safe initial guidance:
- Treat all gates as open; minimum crossing distance to the goal is a lower bound
  on remaining actions. If disconnected even in that graph, the goal is impossible.
- Bridges separating start and goal identify mandatory crossings in that relaxed
  graph. A gate on just one promising route is not necessarily mandatory.
- A resource-handling relaxation could bound unavoidable pickups/placements while
  ignoring access restrictions. Its formula remains optional downstream work.
- Combine total-cost lower bounds with max. Add bounds only with a sound argument,
  such as one bounding crossings and another bounding disjoint handling actions.
  Estimates of box delivery that include travel may overlap navigation costs.

Region/milestone decomposition must retain relevant entry/exit, boxes available
or committed, gate status, and cost. Shortest individual segments do not prove a
globally shortest route. Retain relevant alternatives, prove a restriction safe,
or offer the user an explicit tradeoff.

These requirements and safe reductions are structural conclusions. Stronger
bounds, exact algorithms, cache/memory choices, and measured benefit belong to
subsequent specification/search work. No benchmark is required to finish intake.

9. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA FOR THE SPEC-ADVISOR HANDOFF

- Preserve supplied objects/layout, initial locations, gate/plate wiring, and goal.
- Represent local pickup, placement, and crossing as individual unit actions.
- Reject pickup with occupied hands, placement on an occupied plate, crossing a
  closed gate, and cross-area manipulation. Preserve total boxes at every step.
- Moving through a gate leaves its supporting box in place. Picking up that box
  closes the gate immediately. Carried boxes move with the agent.
- Omit plate-step actions under R1; do not ask about carrying while on a plate
  unless a changed scope invalidates that reduction.
- Box label swaps preserve legality and cost. Multiple identical local loose
  boxes must not force distinct equivalent choices in an optimized representation.
- Preserve the cheaper arrival if duplicate sharing is implemented; verify actual
  canonicalization and named paths if symmetry is enabled.
- The supplied instance has the ten-action witness and matching lower bound.
  A later shorter result indicates a rule/action-count discrepancy to investigate.

  and performance measurements. None alone establishes all the others.
