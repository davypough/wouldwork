AUTHORIZATION AND SCOPE
After the prior keeper-resource continuation analysis, D approved developing
the alternative recorder baseline through read-only analysis. This analysis
uses only the continuation prompt, restart checkpoint, permitted generated
profile and evidence, the authored instance, and the recorder, mobility,
placement, and cargo source files. No Lisp was executed. No checkpoint was
imported, staged, replayed, searched, or validated. No extractor, engine, or
ledger code was changed, and no sealed material was read.

RESULT: A FRESH TWO-CYCLE BASELINE CANDIDATE
The current depth-32 keeper checkpoint cannot be converted in place: its one
open recorder cycle fixes the ghost arrangement that creates the gate1
obstruction. The alternative is therefore a fresh baseline, not a replacement
for or mutation of any of the three saved checkpoints.

The candidate persistent arrangement before its second recorder start is:

  - tray1 on plate1 at location2;
  - box1 on plate2 at location7;
  - agent1 empty-handed at recorder1's location1;
  - the first recorder cycle explicitly cancelled and therefore ghost-free.

At the second START-RECORDER, tray1* and box1* are forked onto those same
fixed plates. During that open cycle they, rather than agent1*, supply
recording pressure for gate1 and gate3. The physical view is also held open
by their live counterparts. This gives the new ghost agent a route from R1
through gate1 to R8 and through gate3 to R2, where connector1* is located.

SOURCE-DERIVED CONSTRUCTION
1. In the initial state, agent1 starts the first recorder cycle at location1.
   START-RECORDER forks agent1* at location1 and tray1* on plate2.
2. Agent1* can make its explicit local STEP onto plate1 at location2. That
   makes recording gate1 and physical gate1 open. The later dismount remains
   prohibited as the original gate1 obstruction states; this candidate does
   not require it.
3. While agent1* remains on plate1, agent1 can use physical gate1 to reach
   tray1 at plate2, pick it up, carry it back through physical gate1, and put
   it on plate1. Live and ghost occupants may share a support during an open
   recorder cycle, so the live tray placement does not conflict with agent1*.
4. Agent1 returns to recorder1 empty-handed and uses CANCEL-PLAYBACK. This
   is a legal cycle ending independent of the ghost's return. It removes the
   ghosts and seeds the closed recording shadow from the surviving live state.
   Thus tray1 now supplies both physical and closed-recording pressure on
   plate1.
5. With tray1 retaining physical gate1, agent1 can reach R8, traverse the
   initially open inverted gate2 to location6, retrieve box1, return through
   gate2, and put box1 on plate2 at location7. The jump and cargo rules place
   no general empty-handed restriction on gate2; its gate must merely be
   open. The authored initial switch1 setting leaves gate2 open.
6. Agent1 returns to recorder1 empty-handed and starts the second cycle.
   START-RECORDER's snapshot fork preserves tray1* on plate1 and box1* on
   plate2. Recording gate1 and gate3 are therefore open for the new ghost
   agent without making it a plate witness.

WHY THIS ADDRESSES THE NEWLY FOUND OBSTRUCTION
The prior candidate relied on agent1* itself for recording gate1. Its required
STEP down removed the sole ghost pressure before its next ground MOVE, closing
the very gate it needed to cross. Here tray1* remains on plate1 when the new
ghost leaves location2, so that dismount does not close recording gate1.
Unlike a live replacement during the prior open cycle, the cargo witness is
present in the recording view because it was forked there at the second start.

PLATE2 AND CLOSURE ACCOUNTING
The first cycle begins with tray1* on plate2, but it is not relied upon as the
second cycle's gate3 witness. CANCELLING that first cycle removes the ghost
tray. Moving box1 to plate2 before the second start makes box1* the independent
recording witness at its fork. This avoids trading the gate1 obstruction for a
closed recording gate3 that would leave connector1* inaccessible in R2.

CANCEL-PLAYBACK requires a live, empty-handed agent at a recorder and then
atomically removes all ghost references. It does not require the ghost to
return. The first cycle has persistent live progress -- tray1 has moved from
plate2 to plate1 -- so this is not the recorder's no-progress case. The second
START-RECORDER requires precisely the resulting closed, ghost-free boundary
and is allowed because this problem has no maximum recorder-cycle limit.

UNRESOLVED OBLIGATIONS
This is a baseline arrangement, not a concrete solution, a replayable
sequence, or a search recommendation.

  - Source restoration remains separate. The new ghost can reach plate3 in R2
    and could be a physical gate4 witness while the live connector is paired
    at location9, but no action sequence or simultaneous endpoint has been
    measured.
  - Tray1 and box1 are committed to plate1 and plate2. They cannot also be
    assumed to support gate6 or an elevated relay. The two cutoff-12 bounds do
    not determine whether another assignment resolves that later shortage.
  - The candidate preserves the existing gate1/gate3 route for the new ghost;
    it does not establish legal transport of ghost connector1*, a gate6
    crossing, switch2 access, gate8 power, source restoration, or the final
    goal.
  - Any next operation must state a concrete endpoint, preserved witnesses,
    and a bounded purpose, then receive separate approval. No cutoff or command
    is recommended from this analysis.
